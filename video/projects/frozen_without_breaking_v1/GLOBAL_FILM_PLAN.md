# Global Film Plan — *Frozen Without Breaking*

## Film lock

**Title:** *Frozen Without Breaking*
**Subtitle:** *How the U.S. economy kept growing as job creation and worker mobility stalled*
**Locked duration:** 14:08.367 at 30 fps
**Editorial chapters:** 7
**Central question:** How can payrolls fall, unemployment fall, and the private economy keep expanding at the same time?
**Thesis:** The United States is not in a broad layoff collapse; it is in a low-hire, low-quit, low-fire freeze in which participation is retreating, health care carries more than all net job growth, and productivity lets output expand with almost no additional labor hours even as private demand remains strong—leaving workers with less mobility and policymakers with no clean exit.

**Viewer promise:** By the end, a cold sophisticated viewer will understand why 4.1 percent unemployment can coexist with stalled job creation; why low layoffs do not make the market healthy; how output and private demand can keep growing; who does not necessarily share that resilience; and which future evidence would mean thaw, continued freeze, or a true break.

The film is one tightening argument, not seven repeated packages. Chapter divisions guide authorship and render review; only the title and a few quiet act turns need appear on screen.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. **The factual spine:** BLS Employment Situation/CES/CPS, BLS JOLTS, Department of Labor claims, BLS Productivity and Costs, ECI, CPI and Real Earnings.
2. **Macroeconomic context:** BEA GDP and real final sales to private domestic purchasers.
3. **Dated policy authority:** the July 29 FOMC statement, transcript, and seal-free chair-only video excerpts. These establish what the Fed said and when; they are not proof of the film’s interpretation.
4. **Qualitative texture:** the Beige Book can corroborate low-hire/low-fire conditions but never carries a national numeric conclusion.
5. **Illustrative material:** Pexels footage and generated conceptual images provide texture and metaphor only. They never depict the workers, places, or events measured by the July data.

Every numeric visual is direct-labeled with its period and unit. The January 2026 CPS population-control break is never animated as economic movement. June JOLTS is identified as June. The Q2 productivity and 52.9 percent labor-share readings are identified as preliminary. The Fed remarks are visibly dated before the August 7 release.

## Whole-film progression

The narrator identifies both the one-month uncertainty and the multi-source alignment that makes the trajectory meaningful. The title arrives over `frozen_concourse_imagegen_v1.png`, with subtle light/trail movement, then resolves into real commuter flow.

**Viewer turn:** One report did not simply announce July. It rewrote the recent past.
**Re-hook:** “Which number is telling the truth? They can all be true.”

### 2. The Rate That Fell Without Hiring — 2:00.500–4:00.967

Resolve the apparent contradiction by distinguishing payroll jobs from household people. Place `4.1%` inside the empty office while household employment `−87k`, labor force `−264k`, and unemployment `−178k` move across the room’s boundary. Participation is down 0.7 point since January; the employment-population ratio is down 0.5 point.

The concept-adjusted household/payroll divergence appears briefly from February through July, explicitly after the January population-control break. Neither survey is declared false. The visual ends with the rate gone and the chair still empty.

**Viewer turn:** The unemployment rate fell without a household-employment boom.
**Re-hook:** “The market is not simply shedding jobs. It is losing motion.”

### 3. The Revolving Door Stopped — 4:00.967–6:09.833

Change from stocks to flows. Rail-like traces show June hiring `3.4%`, quitting `2.0%`, and layoffs/discharges `1.1%`, all below February 2020. The `1.04` openings-per-unemployed ratio appears only with an immediate mismatch caveat. Claims provide the anti-collapse evidence: 209,000 initial claims in the week ending August 8, lower than the comparable week a year earlier.

Use the real subway, office-wide, empty-office, and short interview details as thresholds, not as fictional worker biographies. The rhythmic pulse loses parts as each flow slows. The phrase “low hire, low quit, low fire” is earned only after all three have appeared.

**Viewer turn:** Stability feels reassuring to an incumbent and closed to an entrant.
**Re-hook:** “If the market is not firing at scale, the next question is where its remaining growth is coming from.”

### 4. One Sector Holds the Ceiling — 6:09.833–7:53.933

Cross-cut retail, financial-paper, warehouse, and distant office fragments. Then convert the figures into architecture: health care `+392k` over the year supports total payroll growth of `+316k`; remove it and all other sectors combined settle at roughly `−76k`.

Pass through `load_bearing_healthcare_imagegen_v1.png`, then into the real nurse clip for an unhurried, lightly scored human breath. Counterweight the dramatic magnitude with the private diffusion index at 51.8 and brief mention of other positive sectors. Retail, financial activities, and local government education appear as selected pressure points, not a league table.

**Viewer turn:** Growth is not universally negative, but its magnitude is unusually concentrated.
**Re-hook:** “So why has an economy with so little labor absorption not stopped?”

### 5. The Machine Keeps Running — 7:53.933–9:42.067

The film’s pace accelerates. Warehouse, office, and grocery motion carry a native output-hours separation: nonfarm-business output `+2.5%` year over year, hours `+0.2%`, productivity `+2.2%`. The gap is shown as arithmetic, not labeled AI or automation. Unit labor costs can appear as a secondary `+1.4%` endpoint if the frame remains clean.

Private demand is felt in transactions and work before two macro anchors appear: Q2 real GDP `+1.5%` annualized and real final sales to private domestic purchasers `+3.9%`. No dual axis, market screen, or dashboard. Exclude the branded self-checkout clip by default because it invites a causal claim the evidence cannot support.

**Viewer turn:** Aggregate resilience and labor weakness are simultaneously real.
**Re-hook:** “The economy’s resilience is real. The next question is who gets to feel it.”

### 6. The Missing Share — 9:42.067–11:28.433

The pace contracts. Earnings `+3.2%` and composition-controlled private wages `+3.1%` approach headline CPI at `+3.4%`; real hourly earnings land at `−0.2%`. Core CPI at `2.5%` complicates the picture. Low quits return as an analytical constraint on mobility and bargaining power, never a proven causal coefficient.

The preliminary 52.9 percent labor-share reading emerges quietly from a trace beginning in 1947. The word `PRELIMINARY` stays attached. The image is an unsettled clue, not a scandal sting. Paper friction, office air, and a single low note replace the productive pulse.

**Viewer turn:** An economy can become more efficient before it becomes more generous.
**Re-hook:** “That split now reaches the institution asked to balance employment and prices.”

### 7. No Easy Exit — 11:28.433–14:08.367

Rebuild the timeline: July 29 rate hold at 3.50–3.75 percent, with three voters preferring a quarter-point increase; August 7 jobs and revisions; August 12 CPI. State explicitly that the later data did not yet exist in the July 29 public record.

J-cut into `FED_20260729_DUAL_MANDATE`. Recommended local use is approximately `00:03.55–00:19.35`, beginning after the conversational preface and ending after “price stability and full employment is an either-or proposition.” The portrait occupies the central third while employment and price traces inhabit surrounding space; his authentic audio can continue as the picture recedes.

The narrator names the bind without forecasting a rate decision: weak hiring raises employment risk; elevated inflation resists an automatic easing story; private demand and productivity complicate both. Three final visual states test the thesis:

- **continued freeze:** payroll growth near zero, low hires/quits, contained claims, weak participation, concentrated growth;
- **thaw:** broader payroll gains, stronger participation, higher hires/quits without more layoffs;
- **break:** claims and layoffs jump and unemployment rises alongside job loss.

Return from the occupied office behind glass to the empty doorway. End with distant transit air and no impact sound.

**Final two lines:** “The headline says the labor market has not broken. But a system with so little motion has less room to absorb whatever comes next.”

Hold for two seconds after the final word.

## Recurring motifs

- **Motion:** commuters, hands, workplace movement, authority gesture; then hires, quits, layoffs, output, and hours.
- **Thresholds:** platform, door, interview chair, frame boundary, labor-force boundary.
- **Absence:** empty room, unused chair, negative space, missing labor hours.
- **Institutional ink:** exact dates, paper, revision ghosts, source words; evidence arrives as something knowable at a particular time.
- **Load:** health care becomes a structural member rather than a colored sector bar.

The motifs evolve rather than repeat. Human flow opens the film. Measured flow diagnoses it. Productive motion briefly surges. The last minute removes motion and leaves space.
