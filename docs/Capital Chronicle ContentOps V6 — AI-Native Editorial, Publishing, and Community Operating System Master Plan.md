# Capital Chronicle ContentOps V6 — AI-Native Editorial, Publishing, and Community Operating System Master Plan

## 0. Executive Decision

Capital Chronicle ContentOps should now pivot from an AI-assisted publishing cockpit into an AI-native editorial, publishing, and community operating system.

The prior direction correctly focused on supervised publishing, payload hashing, platform credential boundaries, exact previews, browser automation constraints, approval gates, and audit records. That safety architecture remains valid. The change is that ContentOps should no longer be understood primarily as a system that helps Jim prepare posts for platforms. It should become the operating layer that creates, adapts, distributes, audits, and learns from Capital Chronicle content across owned media, social platforms, and a growing community surface.

The new product center is not “publish to more places.” The new product center is the full content loop:

```text
Jim idea / source / research context / future artifact
→ AI research and grounding
→ canonical Substack article
→ SEO and editorial refinement
→ platform-native variants
→ Discord community drop
→ Telegram/operator checkpoint
→ webhook/API/browser/manual dispatch
→ public URL and audit record
→ community feedback and questions
→ LLM summary and content backlog
→ next canonical article
```

This loop is the new north star.

Capital Chronicle needs an audience before the final internal-alpha product becomes public. It will not build that audience through sporadic manual posting. It needs a repeatable content machine that turns serious macro thinking, research discipline, source trust, data sufficiency, forecast readiness, uncertainty, and build-in-public progress into durable public distribution. Discord changes the system because it creates a living feedback layer. Instead of merely pushing posts outward, ContentOps can now collect questions, objections, topic requests, research prompts, and community signals. Those signals become inputs to the next content cycle.

The strategic product definition is:

```text
Capital Chronicle ContentOps is an AI-native operating system for serious macro content and community growth. It uses LLMs to research, draft, rewrite, adapt, summarize, and package content; uses deterministic validators to preserve source discipline and safety; uses Substack as canonical long-form authority; uses Discord as the community and feedback loop; uses Telegram as the remote operator/control lane; and uses API, webhook, browser/CDP, or manual fallback adapters for supervised distribution.
```

The product should not become a spam bot, a social engagement agent, a hidden scheduler, a selfbot, a scraper, or a financial signal service. It should be a controlled AI production desk. AI does the labor. ContentOps owns the gates. Jim owns the final authority.

The most important directional decision is this:

```text
Automation is default.
Approval is mandatory.
Discord community feedback becomes a first-class input.
Manual remains fallback, recovery, or override.
Bot-based Discord interaction is deferred until the final product core is stable.
```

Discord webhooks should be built early because they are simple, channel-scoped, and useful. Discord bot/slash commands should be built later after the editorial engine, payload hash, destination binding, outbox, audit, and Discord drop adapter are stable. Browser/CDP can be used for setup and QA, but not for selfbot-style posting through Jim’s user account.

This master plan supersedes the previous manual-first and platform-only mental model. It preserves the same safety primitives while expanding the system into a community flywheel.

## 1. The New North Star

The north star of Capital Chronicle ContentOps V6 is:

```text
One serious idea becomes one canonical article, many platform-native payloads, one Discord community drop, one audit-backed distribution record, and one feedback loop that informs the next idea.
```

The product should feel less like a queue of posts and more like an institutional editorial command center with a community signal layer.

The current product ingredients are already strong:

1. Capital Chronicle has a serious content wedge: source trust, data sufficiency, uncertainty, forecast readiness, and no-signal macro education.
2. Substack is available as the canonical long-form publication.
3. Telegram already works as a remote operator/control lane.
4. Meta, Threads, Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, Substack, Telegram, TikTok, and LinkedIn have credential or placeholder structure.
5. Discord now exists as a configured community server with channels, roles, private operator rooms, and three webhooks.
6. LLM infrastructure exists through 9router and Vertex fallback.
7. Browser/CDP strategy exists for platforms where API is slow, expensive, unstable, or blocked.
8. The final environment format and redacted inventory contract now exist.
9. There is already a discipline around not leaking secrets, not posting without approval, not bypassing payload hashes, and not treating model output as authority.

The next step is to unify all of this into one product model.

ContentOps V6 should produce three kinds of output:

### 1. Public content

This includes Substack articles, Discord drops, Telegram channel posts, X manual posts, LinkedIn posts when available, Threads posts, Facebook Page posts, Instagram captions/media packages, YouTube metadata, and future TikTok metadata. Public content is always grounded, reviewed, and safe.

### 2. Operator artifacts

This includes exact previews, payload hashes, approval packets, browser checkpoints, outbox records, redacted audit logs, manual fallback packages, credential capability matrices, platform readiness records, and final evidence packets.

### 3. Community intelligence

This includes Discord questions, feedback summaries, recurring objections, source requests, content ideas, community interest clusters, product confusion signals, and future topic backlog items.

The V6 product is successful when Jim can give one instruction such as:

```text
Write a serious Capital Chronicle piece on why “data is missing” is often the correct macro answer, adapt it for Discord, Substack, Telegram, LinkedIn, and Threads, and prepare the approval packet.
```

Then ContentOps should produce:

```text
- research grounding packet
- canonical Substack article
- SEO title/slug/meta packet
- Discord drop
- Telegram summary
- LinkedIn professional post
- Threads short-form version
- media concept and rights manifest
- draft inspection report
- exact payload previews
- payload hash records
- approval queue entry
- outbox entries for selected channels
- audit-ready evidence packet
```

Jim’s job should be to review, approve, reject, or refine. Jim should not be the default mechanical publisher, copy adapter, image rights checker, webhook payload formatter, or audit recorder.

## 2. Strategic Product Thesis

Capital Chronicle is not trying to win by being louder than finance influencers. It should win by being more disciplined. Most finance content reduces uncertainty into fake confidence. Capital Chronicle should do the opposite: it should make uncertainty legible, source quality inspectable, and forecast readiness conditional.

The content wedge is:

```text
Capital Chronicle explains why source trust, missing data, degraded data, stale data, proxy data, and forecast readiness matter before anyone should pretend to know what a market event means.
```

This wedge can generate many content types:

* educational macro explainers;
* source trust breakdowns;
* build-in-public updates;
* data sufficiency cards;
* no-signal philosophy;
* policy and economics commentary;
* research workflow essays;
* AI tooling behind-the-scenes posts;
* community Q&A;
* failed forecast readiness examples;
* internal alpha readiness narratives later;
* platform-specific short-form derivatives.

The mistake would be to treat this as a writing task. It is not just writing. It is a production system.

The production system has several stages:

```text
Discover → Ground → Draft → Inspect → Adapt → Package → Approve → Dispatch → Audit → Learn
```

LLMs should be used heavily in Discovery, Drafting, Adapting, Packaging, Explaining, Summarizing, and Learning. Deterministic code should control Validation, Hashing, Binding, Approval State, Outbox, Credential Scope, Live Dispatch Eligibility, and Audit. Browser/CDP should be used as a practical adapter when platform APIs are not worth the friction, but it should never become an opaque authority. Discord webhooks should be used early because they make distribution to owned community channels simple. Discord bot/slash commands should be deferred until the final product has a mature core and the community has real interaction volume.

The thesis becomes:

```text
The fastest path to a real ContentOps product is not to build every social API perfectly. It is to build the AI production loop, the canonical article flow, the Discord community drop, the payload approval/outbox/audit system, and the first few practical dispatch adapters.
```

This means the near-term shipping path should prioritize:

1. AI Writer and research grounding.
2. Substack canonical article.
3. Discord webhook drops.
4. Telegram operator/dispatch.
5. Platform variant generation.
6. Browser/CDP adapter for Substack and selected platforms.
7. Metrics and community feedback summary.
8. UI that shows the entire loop.

The system should not block on LinkedIn business verification, X API credentials, TikTok review, or Discord bot implementation. Those are capability expansions, not core product dependencies.

## 3. Platform Roles Under V6

V6 needs a clear platform taxonomy.

### Substack — Canonical Long-Form Authority

Substack is the canonical publication layer. Every serious content item should ideally have a canonical article or newsletter issue. Substack is where arguments can be fully expressed, caveats can be preserved, sources can be explained, and the Capital Chronicle voice can mature.

Substack outputs:

* long-form article;
* title and subtitle;
* SEO slug;
* source/citation notes;
* limitations section;
* no-advice/no-signal disclosure;
* hero image or visual card;
* tags;
* canonical URL;
* derivative brief for other platforms.

Substack should be the source object for downstream variants. Discord, Telegram, LinkedIn, X, Threads, Facebook, Instagram, and YouTube should usually point back to the canonical Substack artifact.

### Discord — Community and Feedback Flywheel

Discord is not merely another publishing destination. It is the community layer.

Discord roles:

* public community home;
* Substack drop distribution;
* build-in-public update surface;
* research question intake;
* content idea intake;
* feedback and objection collection;
* future role-gated rooms;
* future subscriber/community layer;
* private operator/audit/checkpoint bridge.

The current server is already structured for this. It contains start-here channels, official updates, research/macro channels, content/AI channels, community channels, and private operator channels. This is enough for Phase 1.

Discord should begin as webhook-first. The three initial webhook dispatch lanes are:

```text
Capital Chronicle Announcements → #announcements
Capital Chronicle Substack Drops → #substack-drops
Capital Chronicle Product Updates → #product-updates
```

The system should later add Discord bot/slash commands for structured community intake, but not immediately. Phase 1 can use manual community reading plus optional LLM summarization of operator-provided text.

Discord is the reason the content loop changes from one-way publishing into a feedback loop.

### Telegram — Remote Operator and Channel Dispatch

Telegram remains the remote operator/control lane. It already has a working bot/channel model, and it is better suited than Discord for compact operator alerts and mobile approval prompts.

Telegram should handle:

* operator status;
* draft preview summaries;
* approval challenges;
* dispatch result summaries;
* failure notifications;
* manual fallback alerts;
* optional channel broadcast.

Telegram should not become a command shell with arbitrary execution. It should remain a structured remote UI.

### X — Manual First, Automation Later

X should be deferred as API credentials are not the priority and user has chosen manual posting for now. ContentOps should still generate X payloads:

* single post;
* thread;
* image post plan;
* Substack link post;
* no-advice/no-signal safe version.

Execution should be manual until X credentials or browser workflow become worth adding. This keeps shipping velocity high.

### LinkedIn — Personal Later, Business Page Later

LinkedIn personal can be browser/CDP or manual later. Business page is blocked until identity verification and page creation. ContentOps should still generate LinkedIn variants because they are useful for editorial quality.

Execution is deferred. The product should not block on it.

### TikTok — Deferred

TikTok should be treated as a later media-heavy lane. App credentials exist or may exist, but tokens and review are not enough to make it important now. ContentOps can generate TikTok metadata later, but video workflows are not a near-term dependency.

### Meta Family — Facebook, Instagram, Threads

Meta family should be capability-gated:

* Facebook Page: page post or link post.
* Instagram: media-first package requiring rights manifest, image, caption, alt text, crop considerations.
* Threads: short-form derivative posts.

These lanes should be included in platform variant generation, but live dispatch should be controlled by credential proof, app scope proof, payload hash, destination binding, and outbox readiness.

### YouTube — Later Media Asset Channel

YouTube should be treated as a future long-form/video metadata lane. It is not core for immediate ContentOps final product unless video production becomes active. The system can prepare titles, descriptions, chapters, tags, thumbnails, and upload packets later.

## 4. Core Product Architecture

The V6 architecture has sixteen layers.

### Layer 1 — Operator Intent

Inputs can come from:

* Jim entering a topic;
* Jim pasting a source;
* Jim forwarding a Discord community question;
* Jim sending a Telegram operator command;
* a Substack article idea;
* a build-in-public milestone;
* future Capital Chronicle internal-alpha artifact;
* manual content backlog item;
* media prompt;
* platform-specific request.

Operator intent is not approval. It creates a work item.

Intent classes:

```yaml
operator_intent:
  create_canonical_article
  summarize_source
  create_discord_drop
  create_platform_variants
  create_product_update
  create_research_question_backlog
  inspect_draft
  approve_payload
  reject_payload
  request_manual_fallback
  request_browser_checkpoint
  request_webhook_dispatch
  request_audit_summary
```

The system must distinguish “Jim wants a draft” from “Jim approves this exact payload.” Natural language can start workflow, but cannot bypass final approval.

### Layer 2 — Research Grounding

The AI Research Agent transforms topics into a grounded packet. It should identify what is known, what is missing, what source types are available, what claims are safe, and what claims are unsafe.

Research packet fields:

```yaml
research_grounding_packet:
  packet_id
  topic
  source_mode
  source_refs
  official_source_refs
  non_official_source_refs
  freshness_status
  source_quality_status
  missing_evidence
  safe_angles
  unsafe_angles
  required_caveats
  no_signal_status
  no_advice_status
  allowed_for_drafting
  allowed_for_publication
  blocked_reasons
```

LLM can summarize sources, but it must not invent facts, source IDs, metrics, or model outputs.

### Layer 3 — Canonical Substack Article

The canonical article is the primary content object.

It includes:

```yaml
canonical_article:
  article_id
  research_packet_id
  title
  subtitle
  slug_candidate
  lede
  body_markdown
  section_map
  citations
  limitations
  disclosure
  media_request
  seo_packet_id
  draft_status
  human_review_required
```

The article is not public-ready until inspection passes and Jim approves it.

### Layer 4 — SEO and Editorial Optimization

The SEO/editorial agent improves:

* title;
* subtitle;
* slug;
* meta description;
* hook;
* structure;
* readability;
* platform adaptation brief;
* pull quotes;
* summary blocks.

It may not remove uncertainty or caveats.

SEO packet:

```yaml
seo_editorial_packet:
  packet_id
  article_id
  primary_keyword
  secondary_keywords
  search_intent
  title_candidates
  subtitle_candidates
  slug_candidates
  meta_description
  readability_score
  editorial_score
  audience_fit_scores
  rejected_clickbait
  limitations_preserved
```

### Layer 5 — Media and Visual Strategy

Media is central to distribution, especially Discord, Instagram, LinkedIn, Substack, and X. V6 should avoid random image usage. It should prefer two media lanes:

1. Internal visual cards.
2. Rights-checked external media.

Internal visual cards are especially strong for Capital Chronicle because they can express:

* data sufficiency ladder;
* source quality matrix;
* forecast readiness blocked state;
* evidence checklist;
* uncertainty map;
* no-signal explainer;
* article quote card;
* build-in-public milestone card.

Media manifest:

```yaml
media_rights_manifest:
  manifest_id
  article_id
  media_items
  local_file_refs
  license_status
  attribution_status
  platform_constraints
  alt_text
  approved_platforms
  blocked_reasons
```

Media must never contain secrets, raw vendor data, fake market numbers, fake readiness states, or misleading investment implications.

### Layer 6 — Platform Variant Generator

The platform variant agent adapts the canonical article to each target. It should not merely truncate text. Each platform has a grammar.

Variant set:

```yaml
platform_variant_set:
  variant_set_id
  canonical_article_id
  variants:
    substack
    discord_drop
    telegram_post
    x_manual_post
    linkedin_personal
    linkedin_org_future
    threads
    facebook_page
    instagram_caption
    youtube_metadata_future
    tiktok_metadata_deferred
  limitations_preserved
  source_refs_preserved
  no_advice_status
  no_signal_status
  human_review_required
```

Platform variant generation should be available even when dispatch is deferred. Execution readiness is separate from content generation.

### Layer 7 — Discord Drop Adapter

The Discord Drop Adapter is a new first-class object.

A Discord drop is not the same as a tweet or Telegram message. It should be structured for community discussion:

```yaml
discord_drop:
  drop_id
  source_article_id
  target_channel
  title
  one_line_thesis
  why_it_matters
  summary
  discussion_question
  source_link
  disclosure
  embed_payload
  webhook_target
  payload_hash
  approval_required
```

Default format:

```text
Title:
One-line thesis:
Why it matters:
Read the full article:
Discussion question:
```

Discord drops should be written to create discussion, not just broadcast links. The discussion question is important. It turns the server into a feedback loop.

### Layer 8 — Community Signal Agent

The Community Signal Agent converts Discord activity into structured content intelligence.

In Phase 1, it should not require a bot. Jim can manually paste selected questions, or ContentOps can ingest operator-provided exports. Later, a bot/slash command can structure input.

Signal packet:

```yaml
community_signal_packet:
  packet_id
  source
  channel_id
  input_mode
  question_text
  theme
  urgency
  content_potential
  required_sources
  safe_angle
  unsafe_angle
  recommended_next_action
  backlog_candidate
```

Feedback summary:

```yaml
discord_feedback_summary:
  summary_id
  date_range
  source_channels
  recurring_questions
  objections
  confusing_terms
  requested_topics
  product_feedback
  moderation_flags
  recommended_content_backlog
```

This layer is the strategic advantage of Discord. Instead of guessing content topics, Capital Chronicle can listen to what the community asks.

### Layer 9 — Draft Inspector

Draft Inspector validates content before approval.

Checks:

* unsupported claims;
* missing citations;
* no-advice violations;
* no-signal violations;
* source freshness;
* market prediction language;
* model authority leakage;
* trade/execution language;
* content lane mismatch;
* platform constraint violations;
* media rights status;
* Discord tone;
* community safety;
* disclosure presence.

Draft inspection output:

```yaml
draft_inspection_report:
  report_id
  source_content_id
  claim_risk_status
  citation_status
  no_advice_status
  no_signal_status
  platform_constraint_status
  media_rights_status
  discord_safety_status
  approval_eligible_targets
  blocked_targets
  required_edits
  warnings
```

LLM can explain Draft Inspector results, but deterministic validators own pass/block state.

### Layer 10 — Payload Preview and Hash

Every dispatchable payload must have an exact preview and a payload hash.

Hash inputs include:

* platform;
* destination binding;
* credential handle;
* adapter type;
* text;
* title;
* media manifest hash;
* alt text;
* visibility;
* source refs;
* policy snapshot;
* draft inspection status;
* approval status;
* adapter version.

The hash must not include raw tokens, cookies, session data, localStorage, passwords, browser profile data, webhook URL values, raw secret file paths, or secret digests.

Payload preview should render the exact content that will be posted or exported.

### Layer 11 — Approval Ledger

Approval is exact and append-only.

Approval binds:

```yaml
approval_ledger_entry:
  approval_id
  operator_id
  payload_id
  payload_hash
  destination_binding_id
  credential_handle_id
  adapter_type
  media_manifest_hash
  approved_at
  expires_at
  revoked
  valid_for_outbox
  valid_for_dispatch
```

Any change invalidates approval:

* text edit;
* platform change;
* destination change;
* webhook target change;
* media change;
* image order change;
* alt text change;
* visibility change;
* adapter version change;
* policy snapshot change;
* source refs change.

### Layer 12 — Dispatch Outbox

Approval creates outbox candidates. It does not directly post.

Outbox entry:

```yaml
dispatch_outbox_entry:
  outbox_id
  platform
  destination_binding_id
  credential_handle_id
  payload_id
  payload_hash
  adapter_type
  approval_id
  idempotency_key
  request_budget
  auto_retry_allowed
  kill_switch_required
  pre_dispatch_revalidation_status
  status
  blocked_reasons
```

For Discord webhooks, request budget should normally be one. Auto-retry should be false unless the failure is clearly pre-request and no post could have occurred.

### Layer 13 — Dispatch Adapters

V6 supports four adapter classes:

```yaml
dispatch_adapter:
  webhook_adapter
  official_api_adapter
  browser_cdp_adapter
  manual_fallback_adapter
```

Webhook adapter:

* Discord announcement webhook;
* Discord Substack drop webhook;
* Discord product update webhook.

Official API adapter:

* Telegram Bot API;
* Meta/Threads/Facebook/Instagram where scopes pass;
* YouTube later;
* TikTok later.

Browser/CDP adapter:

* Substack compose/publish;
* LinkedIn personal later;
* platforms where API is blocked;
* setup and QA for Discord.

Manual fallback adapter:

* X manual;
* LinkedIn manual until verified;
* TikTok deferred;
* any platform with uncertain UI or credentials.

### Layer 14 — Browser Automation Plan

Browser/CDP remains important, but it is not used for Discord selfbot posting.

Browser plan object:

```yaml
browser_automation_plan:
  plan_id
  platform
  objective
  browser_profile_id
  target_url
  destination_binding_id
  credential_handle_id
  payload_id
  payload_hash
  allowed_actions
  forbidden_actions
  stop_conditions
  requires_pre_click_checkpoint
  requires_jim_go
  request_budget
  auto_retry_allowed
```

For Discord, browser/CDP is allowed for:

* server setup;
* channel verification;
* webhook creation;
* visual QA;
* manual copy of IDs.

It is not allowed for:

* user account automation;
* selfbot posting;
* reading user token;
* cookies/localStorage/sessionStorage;
* DMs;
* scraping user discussions.

### Layer 15 — Audit and Evidence Vault

Every important transition is recorded.

Audit events:

```yaml
automation_audit_event:
  event_id
  event_type
  platform
  adapter_type
  destination_binding_id
  credential_handle_id
  payload_hash
  response_class
  public_url
  webhook_message_id_if_available
  screenshot_refs
  no_secret_output_verified
  retry_count
  manual_fallback_required
  audit_hash
```

Discord audit should record:

* webhook target name;
* channel ID;
* payload hash;
* message result class;
* public URL if available;
* no webhook URL persisted in audit;
* no raw webhook token;
* no cookie/session data.

### Layer 16 — Metrics and Learning

Metrics should initially be manual or API-provided where safe. No scraping by default.

Metrics records:

```yaml
metrics_record:
  metrics_id
  platform
  public_url
  content_id
  posted_at
  source
  impressions
  reactions
  comments
  clicks
  replies
  saves
  notes
  missing_metrics
```

Discord learning should include:

* reactions on drops;
* questions asked;
* topics repeated;
* confusion signals;
* feedback quality;
* suggested next article.

Metrics do not need to be perfect early. The first goal is to close the content loop.

Do not build moderation automation early. Do not build autonomous reply agents. Do not DM users automatically.

research question generation

title and hook generation
platform adaptation
Discord drop writing
community question clustering

cannot hide blockers

llm_output_packet:

LLM proposes. Validators constrain. Jim approves. Adapters execute. Audit records.

## 9. Security and Safety Model

V6 is faster and more automated, so the security model must remain strict.

Forbidden:

```text
raw token output
webhook URL in logs
cookie dump
localStorage/sessionStorage dump
browser user token
Discord selfbot
autonomous replies
DM automation
unapproved live write
hidden scheduler
scraping private messages
financial advice
buy/sell/hold
price targets
position sizing
guaranteed predictions
fake model outputs
fake readiness
fake data
```

Required:

```text
credential handles
destination bindings
payload hashes
approval ledger
outbox
idempotency
kill switch
redacted audit
manual fallback
no-secret tests
platform capability matrix
```

Discord-specific policy:

```text
Webhook dispatch allowed only to configured channel bindings.
Webhook URLs are secrets.
Discord user account automation is forbidden.
Discord bot deferred until product core is stable.
Slash commands preferred over broad message content ingestion.
Private operator channel content must remain redacted.
No public posting from private audit details.
```

Browser/CDP policy:

```text
Allowed for setup, QA, compose flows, and approved supervised actions.
Forbidden for credential extraction, cookies, tokens, selfbot behavior, DMs, and unapproved posting.
```

LLM policy:

```text
LLM can draft, summarize, rewrite, classify, and explain.
LLM cannot approve, dispatch, certify truth, hide blockers, or create authority.
```

## 13. Final Operating Principle

Capital Chronicle ContentOps V6 should be built as an AI-native content and community desk, not a generic dashboard and not a bot farm.

The product should let Jim operate at a higher level:

```text
Choose the idea.
Review the evidence.
Approve the payload.
Watch the system distribute.
Learn from the community.
Decide the next move.
```

Everything else should be automated, validated, or prepared for fallback.

The final posture is:

```text
Substack is the canonical article.
Discord is the community flywheel.
Telegram is the operator lane.
LLMs are the production engine.
Webhooks are the first Discord live adapter.
APIs are used where practical.
Browser/CDP is a supervised adapter, not a secret-reading agent.
Manual is fallback.
Audit is permanent.
Jim remains authority.
```

The reason this approach is stronger than the previous one is that it creates compounding learning. A platform-only publishing system can post content. A community operating system can discover what content should exist next. That is the difference between a content scheduler and a real editorial engine.

Capital Chronicle should not merely publish. It should listen, learn, and turn serious research discipline into a visible community habit.
