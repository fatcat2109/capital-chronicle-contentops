US consumer inflation turned higher in July after declining in June, although the annual rate moved below its prior reading.

The Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers rose 0.1 percent on a seasonally adjusted basis after falling 0.4 percent in June. Over the 12 months ending in July, the all-items index increased 3.4 percent, following a 3.5 percent rise for the period ending in June. [www.bls.gov](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/pdf/cpi.pdf)

## Shelter leads the monthly increase

Shelter rose 0.1 percent and accounted for roughly two-thirds of the monthly all-items increase. Food also advanced 0.1 percent, with food away from home up 0.3 percent. Energy moved in the opposite direction, declining 1.5 percent as gasoline fell 2.9 percent. [www.bls.gov](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/pdf/cpi.pdf)

Core CPI, which excludes food and energy, increased 0.2 percent after being unchanged in June. Its 12-month increase was 2.5 percent, following a 2.6 percent rise for the period ending in June. [www.bls.gov](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/pdf/cpi.pdf)

The component mix leaves a clear distinction between the headline number and its drivers: shelter supplied most of the monthly increase, while falling energy costs restrained it. On an annual basis, energy was still up 14.7 percent and food was up 3.0 percent. [www.bls.gov](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/pdf/cpi.pdf)
