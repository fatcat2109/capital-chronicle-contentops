# Al Jazeera Reports: Caracas Mulls OPEC Exit as Trump Closes In on Deal for Venezuela's Oil Fields

SINGLE-SOURCE REPORT - ALJAZEERA: Al Jazeera reports that Caracas is mulling an OPEC exit as the Trump administration closes in on a deal to secure stakes in Venezuelan oil fields.

According to a SINGLE-SOURCE REPORT - ALJAZEERA, the US is working on a deal to secure long-term access to Venezuelan oil fields, while Caracas is reportedly mulling an exit from OPEC. If these unconfirmed reports are accurate, the potential agreement could reshape global energy geopolitics by locking in US access to the world's largest crude reserves while weakening OPEC's influence. However, because these developments remain unconfirmed, any immediate impact on global oil markets or US energy security depends entirely on whether the deal is finalized and survives potential legal challenges. [[SOURCE:SOURCE_1]]

The reported deal, which insiders said could be signed and made public soon, is expected to operate under a legal model where the US government would secure a group of Venezuelan oilfields to be developed by American companies, with the resulting supply guaranteed for the US. A source cited in the report indicated that a "lease" is under consideration as the legal model, with a further auction or tender to allocate each of the fields among US producers. A list of 17 fields in negotiation reportedly includes green fields in the vast Orinoco Belt, combined with mature areas in Lake Maracaibo, some of which are currently operated by a small Chinese firm whose contract was signed during the administration of former President Nicolas Maduro. [[SOURCE:SOURCE_1]]

The Al Jazeera report notes that the Venezuelan government has for decades prevented foreign producers from booking the country's oil reserves, and current hydrocarbons regulation does not include acreage leases for oil areas, while the constitution reserves the industry's core activities to the state. Although recently reformed oil legislation allows oilfield operation through joint ventures and production-sharing contracts, experts cited in the report warn that the prospective deal could raise constitutional questions and face legal challenges. Capital Chronicle's analysis suggests that these legal hurdles represent a primary watch condition; if Venezuelan courts or political factions challenge the constitutionality of the "lease" model, the entire agreement could be delayed or derailed. [[SOURCE:SOURCE_1]]

The geopolitical implications are further heightened by reports, originally published by Bloomberg and cited by Al Jazeera, that Caracas is considering leaving the OPEC oil production group as it strengthens ties with the US. The Al Jazeera report notes that Venezuela, a founding member of OPEC in 1960, has not met its production quotas for years due to neglect and corruption in its state-run oil industry, while Washington has long been at odds with OPEC over its influence on global oil prices. Capital Chronicle's interpretation is that an OPEC exit would allow Venezuela to align its production directly with US demand without being bound by cartel quotas, though the actual impact on global supply would depend on how quickly Venezuela's deteriorated energy industry—which currently produces some 1.25 million barrels per day of crude—can be rehabilitated. [[SOURCE:SOURCE_1]]

For the Trump administration, securing Venezuelan crude is a high-stakes domestic priority. According to the Al Jazeera report, since the US reportedly captured and removed former President Nicolas Maduro from power in January, Washington has been trying to secure a stable flow of Venezuelan crude for US refineries while promoting American investment into the country’s deteriorated energy industry, which currently produces some 1.25 million barrels per day of crude. The report notes that the administration is under pressure ahead of November's midterm elections over rising petrol prices, and it has also been looking for solutions to replenish its Strategic Petroleum Reserve. Funding shortages and ongoing maintenance have reportedly limited efforts to refill the reserve after the US tapped it following Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022 and again after a reported war on Iran was launched in February.
