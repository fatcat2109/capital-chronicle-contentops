# Meta's Q3 results show investment outrunning cash generation

Operating cash flow rose, yet Meta posted a $2.12 billion free-cash-flow deficit. A large legal charge clouded the profit comparison.

In its [October 21 earnings release](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx), Meta reported $32.79 billion of operating cash flow for the third quarter, up from $30.00 billion a year earlier, but a $2.12 billion free-cash-flow deficit. Revenue grew 26% to $64.60 billion. The results expose the cost of investing faster than operating cash generation can keep up.

The earnings comparison is less straightforward: a $9.89 billion litigation charge weighed on GAAP operating profit, and Meta illustrates a stronger result with that charge removed. The distinction matters for investors: a profit adjustment cannot by itself turn an investment cash outflow into cash generated by the business.

## The investment bill overtakes operating cash

Property and equipment purchases reached $33.942 billion, against $18.829 billion a year earlier. Finance-lease principal payments added $964 million, up from $545 million. Together, those payments exceeded the $32.786 billion generated by operations, leaving negative free cash flow of $2.120 billion. The same calculation a year earlier left positive free cash flow of $10.625 billion.

The squeeze was already visible in the second quarter. Meta's [July 29 results](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx) show operating cash flow of $31.862 billion, up from $25.561 billion a year earlier. Equipment purchases of $30.116 billion and finance-lease principal of $962 million left just $784 million of free cash flow, against $8.549 billion in Q2 2025.

Free cash flow is Meta's non-GAAP measure, calculated after both equipment purchases and finance-lease principal payments. Its headline capital-expenditure figure already includes those lease payments; subtracting them again from that figure would double count them. This measure also differs from the total change in cash and does not represent all cash available for discretionary spending.

Operating cash generation rose in both comparisons, but equipment purchases and finance-lease payments absorbed more of it. That is a cash-coverage problem at the current spending rate. It does not establish how much of the spending was for AI or what return those assets will earn.

## The legal charge changes the profit comparison

GAAP operating income fell to $12.988 billion from $20.535 billion in the third quarter; the reported operating margin fell to 20% from 40%. The company included a $9.89 billion charge for its August agreement with state attorneys general over litigation concerning teen mental health and social media.

Meta says operating income would have been $22.88 billion without that charge, above the prior-year GAAP result. That is a serious countercase to reading the reported decline as a collapse in the operating business. It is also the company's chosen adjustment: the legal obligation remains an economic cost.

The charge is already inside reported expenses; it must not be deducted from operating profit a second time. Removing it from earnings is not evidence of a cash receipt or an equivalent increase in free cash flow. Recognizing an expense and paying the resulting obligation can fall in different periods.

The earlier quarter also included $2.40 billion of legal charges and $1.18 billion of severance expenses. A legal-charge-only add-back therefore does not produce a consistently normalized profit series. Nor is the rebound in net income a clean shortcut: the effective tax rate was 87% in Q3 2025, versus 24% in Q3 2026.

## What Class C shares change

The October 21 release says the board approved a non-voting Class C share class, intended to take effect on January 19, 2027. A proposed stock dividend would distribute one Class C share for each outstanding Class A or Class B share, conditional on the charter change taking effect. Meta says that distribution will leave shareholders' voting power unchanged.

The company lists capital raising, employee compensation and acquisitions among the potential uses of the class. Future share sales could raise cash, and share-based payments could preserve cash that would otherwise be spent. The announced distribution to existing holders is a different step: a stock dividend does not itself raise cash.

## The test is sustained cash coverage

The strongest countercase is an investment cycle: spending arrives before the benefits. Meta says AI is supporting advertising and that its newer AI investments will take longer to generate returns. Rising operating cash flow leaves that explanation open; these two quarterly comparisons cannot establish a lasting funding crisis.

The next test is whether operating cash generation can again cover equipment purchases and finance-lease principal through comparable periods. Sustained positive free cash flow would weaken the concern about persistent funding strain; repeated deficits would strengthen it if cash generation failed to catch up. Any later cash-raising share issue would need its own proceeds and terms.

That cash test still would not, on its own, establish an AI return. Management says AI is contributing incremental advertising revenue, but these tables do not isolate the spending, cash receipts and time horizon needed to measure the payoff. Evidence tying particular investments to later cash generation would be needed to judge it.
