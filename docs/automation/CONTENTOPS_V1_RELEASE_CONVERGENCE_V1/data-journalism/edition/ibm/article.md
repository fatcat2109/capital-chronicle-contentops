# IBM's software-led rebound faces a broader demand test

IBM's October 21 results show software carrying the revenue recovery. Higher segment profits and free cash flow support the rebound; consulting and gross margins complicate the picture.

IBM's third-quarter results show a recovery with a clear engine: software. In its [October 21 earnings release](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-10-21-IBM-RELEASES-THIRD-QUARTER-RESULTS), the company reported revenue of $17.10 billion, compared with $16.33 billion a year earlier. Software supplied $595 million of that increase, while Consulting added just $11 million.

Capital Chronicle's reading is that IBM has evidence of a stronger software business, with the breadth of a spending recovery still open. Better software segment profitability and higher free cash flow support the rebound. Softer gross margins and near-flat Consulting prevent the sales improvement alone from settling the demand question.

## Software led growth, but the breadth changed

Comparisons with [IBM's April 22 results](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-04-22-IBM-RELEASES-FIRST-QUARTER-RESULTS) and [July 22 results](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-07-22-IBM-RELEASES-SECOND-QUARTER-RESULTS) put the rebound in perspective. Company revenue rose by $1,376 million in Q1, $185 million in Q2 and $772 million in Q3 of 2026, measured against the corresponding 2025 quarters. Software contributed $716 million, $374 million and $595 million respectively.

Infrastructure added $440 million in Q1, subtracted $307 million in Q2 and added $148 million in Q3. Consulting's contribution slowed from $204 million to $13 million and then $11 million. In Q2, Software's increase exceeded IBM's total gain because the other reported segment changes, chiefly Infrastructure's decline, offset part of it.

The comparison measures reported dollars and does not separate currency, acquisitions or deal timing. Same-quarter comparisons help with seasonality, but these three observations cannot establish an organic demand trend or how long the recovery will last.

## Gross margin fell while segment profit margin rose

Software segment profit margin rose to 35.0% in Q3 2026 from 32.9% in Q3 2025, a gain of 2.1 percentage points. The same-quarter improvements were 0.7 points in Q1 and 1.1 points in Q2. On IBM's segment-profit measure, higher sales came with better profitability.

Software gross profit margin moved the other way: 82.5% in Q3 2026 against 83.1% in Q3 2025, down 0.6 percentage points. Its year-on-year declines were 0.8 points in Q1 and 1.3 points in Q2.

IBM reports gross margin and segment profit margin as separate measures. The improvement in segment profit is meaningful, but it does not establish better gross profitability per dollar of software sales. These tables do not identify how much of the divergence came from product mix, acquisition accounting, expense timing or productivity; attributing it to any one driver would go beyond the figures.

The strongest countercase is that software revenue rose in every same-quarter comparison and its segment profit margin improved. An account of the recovery that focuses only on weaker gross margins would miss those gains.

## Operating-cash gains narrowed after IBM's adjustments

For the nine months ended September 30, 2026, IBM reported GAAP operating cash flow of $11,791 million, against $9,153 million in 2025, an increase of $2,638 million. Its own free-cash-flow measure rose by $606 million, from $7,181 million to $7,787 million. The improvement survives IBM's adjustments, but the gain is much smaller.

IBM removes the change in Financing receivables before subtracting net capital spending to calculate free cash flow. A bigger financing adjustment can therefore widen the gap between operating cash growth and free cash growth without proving weaker software demand.

The Financing-receivables adjustment grew from $905 million to $2,805 million, removing $1,900 million more from the operating-cash comparison. Net capital spending increased from $1,067 million to $1,200 million, absorbing a further $133 million. Those two changes account for most of the gap between the cash-growth measures.

Free cash flow is a company-defined non-GAAP measure. IBM's bridge excludes acquisitions, debt movements and dividends, so it is not a complete account of the cash balance or a measure of cash available to shareholders. Nor can consolidated cash flow establish the cash conversion of Software alone.

## The next test is breadth and follow-through

The next test is whether Software keeps its profit improvement, Consulting contributes more and cash gains survive the same financing adjustment. Broader gains would strengthen the demand-recovery case; weaker follow-through would leave the rebound concentrated.
