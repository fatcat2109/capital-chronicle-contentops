# The Fed's September decision: three rates with different jobs

Confirmed September 16, 2026 instructions set reserve-balance interest at 3.90%, standing repo at 4.0% and reverse repo at 3.75%, effective September 17. The settings explain implementation; they do not measure what banks or markets subsequently paid.

The Federal Reserve's confirmed September 16, 2026 implementation note set a 3.90% interest rate on reserve balances, a 4.0% standing overnight repo rate and a 3.75% standing overnight reverse-repo offering rate, all effective September 17. These are announced terms for putting a policy decision into operation, not observations of what banks or markets subsequently paid. [www.federalreserve.gov](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a1.htm)

The companion FOMC statement records a quarter-percentage-point increase in the federal funds target range to 3.75%-4% and continuation of the ample-reserves policy. The implementation note translates that objective into instructions for the Fed's trading Desk. This is a dated explanation of the September decision, not a new policy announcement. [www.federalreserve.gov](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm) [www.federalreserve.gov](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a1.htm)

Three operational roles

Reserve-balance interest is the payment attached to reserve balances. The Board voted unanimously to set it at 3.90%, effective September 17. For a bank holding such balances, the relevant question is what those balances earn; this is distinct from the terms for obtaining or placing cash overnight. [www.federalreserve.gov](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a1.htm)

Capital Chronicle's interpretation is that the three settings address different sides of cash management: remuneration for reserve balances, temporary cash provision through standing repo, and overnight cash placement through reverse repo. That distinction helps explain how the tools serve a shared policy objective; it does not establish that a particular operation occurred or that a customer's bank rate changed. [www.federalreserve.gov](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a1.htm)

The document's explicit objective for the Desk was to maintain the federal funds rate within the target range using open-market operations as necessary. Its 4.0% standing repo rate and 3.75% reverse-repo offering rate are instructions for separate operations. The chart compares those settings with reserve-balance interest for the same effective date; it is a comparison of announced policy terms, not a market-rate history. [www.federalreserve.gov](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a1.htm)

What would establish transmission?

The strongest countercase to treating the announcement as a verdict on borrowing costs is that instructions are not transactions. These documents supply no observed effective funding rates or matched bank deposit and loan rates, so they cannot establish how much of the decision reached bank customers. An account of policy transmission needs those observations as well as the announced settings. [www.federalreserve.gov](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a1.htm) [www.federalreserve.gov](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm)

For bonds, currencies and other assets, Capital Chronicle's view is conditional: even if the tools influence the overnight cost of cash, the documents alone cannot establish the direction, timing or size of an asset-price response. They describe the policy terms, not a measured market reaction. [www.federalreserve.gov](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a1.htm) [www.federalreserve.gov](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm)

The next useful check is an official operation record dated September 17, 2026 or later, compared with the terms announced on September 16: did an operation occur, on which terms, and with what take-up? A later implementation notice could establish changed settings. Neither record alone would settle bank-customer pass-through; that question requires its own dated rate evidence. [www.federalreserve.gov](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a1.htm)
