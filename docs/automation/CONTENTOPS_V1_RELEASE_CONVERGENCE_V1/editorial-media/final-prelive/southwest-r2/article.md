# Reuters reports Southwest cuts 2026 outlook despite quarterly earnings beat

SINGLE-SOURCE REPORT - REUTERS. A retained October 21 report sets quarterly gains against a lower annual forecast. If accurate, fuel pressure tests how durable the improvement is.

Southwest's reported 2026 outlook cut puts a limit on what its stronger quarter can prove. Reuters' retained October 21, 2026 account gives an adjusted earnings forecast of $2.60-$2.85 a share for the year, previously $3.25-$4.25. **SINGLE-SOURCE REPORT - REUTERS.** If accurate, it leaves the durability of operating gains exposed to a less favorable fuel outlook. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/southwest-airlines-cuts-2026-profit-forecast-fuel-costs-spike-2026-10-21/)

The earnings beat and the forecast answer different questions

Reuters puts third-quarter adjusted earnings at 66 cents a share, against analysts' 58-cent expectation and 11 cents a year earlier. Those are completed-quarter earnings and a quarterly consensus comparison. The lower annual range is Southwest's expectation for 2026, as relayed by Reuters; it is not an actual full-year result. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/southwest-airlines-cuts-2026-profit-forecast-fuel-costs-spike-2026-10-21/)

Reported fare gains face the fuel bill

According to Reuters, average passenger fare rose nearly 27% in the third quarter, and revenue reached $8.4 billion, a record for that quarter. The report also says adjusted operating margin improved despite higher fuel costs. These figures do not identify how much of the improvement came from passenger volume or mix. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/southwest-airlines-cuts-2026-profit-forecast-fuel-costs-spike-2026-10-21/)

Reuters puts the fuel bill $821 million above a year earlier. It also relays Southwest's expectation of further jet-fuel price increases in the year's final three months. That expectation belongs to the outlook in the retained report; it is not evidence of what fuel prices later did. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/southwest-airlines-cuts-2026-profit-forecast-fuel-costs-spike-2026-10-21/)

If Reuters' account is accurate, Capital Chronicle's reading is that higher fares can strengthen revenue while a heavier fuel bill absorbs some of the benefit. The quarterly beat shows performance against that quarter's expectations; it does not establish how well the business can absorb the fuel costs anticipated in the annual outlook. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/southwest-airlines-cuts-2026-profit-forecast-fuel-costs-spike-2026-10-21/)

The strongest countercase to a failed-turnaround story is in the same report: adjusted operating margin improved despite higher fuel costs. On that account, operating gains could persist even with a lower annual forecast. The cut alone cannot establish that the turnaround has failed. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/southwest-airlines-cuts-2026-profit-forecast-fuel-costs-spike-2026-10-21/)

The missing earnings bridge is the next test

The retained Reuters account does not supply an earnings bridge separating fares and passenger mix, traffic, nonfuel costs, and fuel price from fuel volume. Without that bridge, assigning the entire forecast reduction to fuel would overstate the evidence. Nor does a higher average fare alone establish stronger demand. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/southwest-airlines-cuts-2026-profit-forecast-fuel-costs-spike-2026-10-21/)

Would later company results show fare and operating gains continuing to absorb the fuel burden? A bridge explaining those contributions, together with later realized margins and earnings, would challenge a cautious reading if it showed the gains holding up; deterioration would strengthen it. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/southwest-airlines-cuts-2026-profit-forecast-fuel-costs-spike-2026-10-21/)
