1/ Is the US economic runway shorter than it looks? While headline data shows resilience, a volatile mix of geopolitical oil shocks, yield curve inversions, and monetary policy lags is threatening the expansion. Let's break down the transmission channels. 🧵

---

2/ Energy shocks hit the economy through two paths. On the demand side, inelastic fuel costs act as a regressive tax, draining household discretionary income. On the supply side, rising crude prices inflate costs for logistics, agriculture, and manufacturing.

---

3/ To offset cost-push inflation, businesses must either absorb lower margins or raise retail prices. When margins compress, corporations typically respond by freezing hiring and reducing capital expenditures, accelerating a cyclical downturn.

---

4/ Meanwhile, the 10Y-2Y Treasury yield curve remains inverted. While massive capital flows into passive ETFs (approaching a projected $2T milestone) may distort long-term yields, ignoring this historical recession warning is risky due to monetary policy lags.

---

5/ The lag of aggressive rate hikes is still working through the banking system. Regional banks, facing higher funding costs, have tightened credit standards. This credit squeeze, combined with volatile energy costs, hits small and medium enterprises hardest.

---

6/ Geopolitical friction is also fragmenting global trade. Rerouting shipping vessels around maritime chokepoints adds weeks to transit times, while trade barriers and tariff debates introduce structural inflation that reduces our capacity to absorb supply shocks.

---

7/ This creates a K-shaped corporate landscape. Tech firms like Seagate, Semtech, and Teradyne thrive on structural AI demand, masking the real margin pressures felt by energy-sensitive sectors like transportation and traditional retail.

---
