Are we looking at inflation all wrong? For decades, macroeconomic theory treated monetary policy and supply-chain logistics as two entirely separate worlds. 

In our latest deep dive for Capital Chronicle, we explore "The Double Squeeze"—the structural convergence of Federal Reserve balance sheet liquidity and physical maritime chokepoints (like the Suez Canal, Panama Canal, and Strait of Hormuz).

Key takeaways from the analysis:

1️⃣ The Liquidity Amplifier: When the Fed maintains an expansive balance sheet (QE), abundant systemic liquidity allows corporations to absorb higher freight costs, build precautionary inventories (the "bullwhip effect"), and easily pass these expenses onto consumers.

2️⃣ The QT Dampener: During Quantitative Tightening, scarce liquidity strips firms of their pricing power. Instead of driving consumer inflation, shipping shocks act as a deflationary tax on corporate profit margins.

3️⃣ Asset Market Re-pricing: This intersection alters traditional correlation models, forcing a rapid repricing of risk premiums across industrials, tech hardware, energy, and alternative digital assets.

Understanding the modern macroeconomic landscape requires looking past superficial market volatility and examining the actual plumbing of global capital and physical trade.
