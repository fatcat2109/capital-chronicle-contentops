How do central bank balance sheets and physical shipping lanes collide to shape global inflation? 🚢💸

In our latest educational deep dive for Capital Chronicle, we explore "The Double Squeeze"—the structural convergence of Federal Reserve liquidity and maritime chokepoints like the Strait of Hormuz, the Bab-el-Mandeb, and the Suez Canal.

When the Fed maintains an expansive balance sheet (QE), abundant liquidity acts as an accelerant. Corporations use cheap credit to hoard inventory, easily passing higher freight costs onto consumers and driving up core inflation.

Conversely, during Quantitative Tightening (QT), scarce liquidity strips firms of their pricing power. Instead of fueling consumer inflation, shipping shocks act as a deflationary tax on corporate profit margins, dragging down earnings and growth.

Understanding modern macroeconomics means looking past superficial market volatility to examine the actual plumbing of global capital and physical trade.
