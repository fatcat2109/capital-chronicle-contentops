## 0. Owner decision

Jim explicitly approves **CONTENTOPS TIER-2 PRO VIDEO FACTORY** as the post-Tier-1 premium media expansion of Capital Chronicle ContentOps.

This decision changes the future product direction for video. Video is no longer an indefinitely deferred or experimental side lane. It is a committed product expansion that begins **only after Tier-1 reaches final acceptance and a new Tier-1 release identity is frozen**.

This North Star does not authorize Tier-2 implementation now, does not change the current Tier-1 next task, and grants no provider, browser, credential, upload, dispatch, publication, or public-write authority.

The current Tier-1 product route remains:

```text
accept/merge the owner-approved 9router V2 runtime lineage

→ major final Tier-1 UI/UX rebuild using real live states
→ Work Package G final full-automation prelaunch run
```

## 1. Product thesis

Tier-2 is **not** a prompt-to-video feature.

Do not build:

```text
prompt
→ one generative-video provider
→ opaque clip
→ upload
```

Build a programmable, evidence-governed AI video factory on top of canonical ContentOps:

```text
canonical selected story + governed evidence package
→ video eligibility / assignment
→ Video Director
→ claim-bound Video Program
→ Chapter Graph + Scene Graph
→ deterministic-first Asset Engine
→ narration / audio
→ programmable composition
→ proxy render
→ deterministic media QA
→ multimodal visual critic
→ bounded scene/chapter revision
→ final long-form + short-form variants
→ immutable Tier-2 package
→ exact-authorized upload / processing / readback / reconciliation
→ video-native performance learning
```

The premium differentiator is **programmable editorial video production**, not dependence on text-to-video generation.

Maps, charts, timelines, filings, official documents, source quotations, kinetic typography, number animation, callouts, comparisons, process diagrams, chapter cards, lower thirds, and branded data storytelling should be produced deterministically from code, governed data, and rights-cleared assets whenever possible.

Generative image or generative-video assets are optional enrichment. The factory must still produce a professional video when generative video is unavailable, too expensive, too slow, low quality, or unsuitable for factual presentation.

## 2. One newsroom, multiple media outputs

Tier-2 must not create a second newsroom, a second analytical authority, or a parallel story-selection system.

The canonical ContentOps story and evidence package remains upstream authority.

Tier-1 and Tier-2 are sibling media outputs:

```text
                    CANONICAL CONTENTOPS STORY
                    + evidence + permissions
                    + claim authority
                    + story/version identity
                    + Capital Chronicle lineage
                              |
               +--------------+--------------+
               |                             |
          TIER-1 MEDIA                    TIER-2 MEDIA
      article/newsletter/social      long-form video + shorts
```

Reuse the same:

- story identity and story version;
- evidence and claim authority;
- source and permission records;
- numeric authority;
- Capital Chronicle analytical authority;
- editorial ranking and no-publication doctrine;
- durable operational state;
- operating modes;
- publication authority;
- incidents and unknown-write rules;
- performance identity and learning lineage.

Tier-2 may transform, explain, visualize, narrate, and distribute governed information. It must not independently create authoritative economic scenarios, Bayesian probabilities, model outputs, forecasts, market regimes, numeric truth, or synthetic source authority.

## 3. Required video lanes

Tier-2 has **two required production lanes**.

### 3.1 `SHORT_FORM_NATIVE`

Purpose: platform-native short-form editorial video for surfaces such as YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels, and compatible future destinations.

The North Star does not hardcode one permanent duration because platform rules and account capabilities change.

Short-form must have its own:

- hook;
- narrative compression;
- pacing;
- scene order;
- information density;
- caption treatment;
- 9:16 composition;
- CTA strategy;
- platform metadata.

A short is **not** a blind center crop of a long-form video.

### 3.2 `LONG_FORM_EDITORIAL_15_45M`

Purpose: professional, premium, evidence-heavy editorial video with a target final runtime of **15 to 45 minutes**.

This is a core Tier-2 product requirement, not an optional future enhancement.

The target product resembles a programmable financial-news explainer / documentary production system rather than a social clip generator.

A long-form production should support:

- chapter-based storytelling;
- multiple evidence sets;
- deeper causal or mechanism explanation;
- Capital Chronicle analysis transformations where authorized;
- deterministic charts and maps;
- document and filing evidence;
- timelines and historical context;
- source cards and attribution;
- professional narration;
- chapter transitions;
- repeatable motion design;
- long-horizon retention QA;
- selective rerender and recovery.

A 2–5 minute video may later exist as an optional mid-form derivative, but it is not the definition of the normal Tier-2 video product.

## 4. Video eligibility and abstention

Not every publishable Tier-1 story deserves video.

Video assignment is a separate downstream decision with explicit success states:

- `VIDEO_SELECTED`
- `VIDEO_DEFERRED`
- `VIDEO_BLOCKED`
- `VIDEO_NOT_SELECTED`

`VIDEO_NOT_SELECTED` is a valid successful outcome.

Video eligibility should consider:

- evidence strength;
- story materiality;
- visualizability;
- narrative depth;
- expected qualified engagement;
- shelf life;
- update-chain stability;
- source and asset rights;
- availability of charts/maps/documents;
- Capital Chronicle analytical richness where applicable;
- recent video concentration;
- production cost;
- render time;
- platform fit.

Never manufacture filler video to hit a volume quota.

## 5. North-star operator experience

The target operator command is conceptually:

> Turn this story into Tier-2 Pro Video.

The system should then run:

```text
Director
→ Producer
→ Asset Engine
→ Narration
→ Renderer
→ Critic
→ Revision
→ Near-final package
```

During early supervised operation, the target is approximately **2–5 meaningful operator interactions per completed video**, not dozens of timeline edits.

The operator should review:

- video eligibility or mode choice when needed;
- meaningful narrative alternatives;
- exceptional rights/provenance blockers;
- material creative differences;
- near-final output.

The operator should not routinely edit raw timestamps, subtitle files, chart coordinates, file paths, render flags, or individual frame positions.

This interaction target must not be converted into a permanent mandatory-human-approval doctrine. `AUTONOMOUS_DEFAULT` remains the product default when future Tier-2 deterministic publication gates are fully authorized and proven.

## 6. Video Director

The Video Director is a bounded editorial/media planning role.

It may decide:

- central narrative question;
- hook;
- long-form chapter structure;
- short-form angle;
- pacing;
- information hierarchy;
- narration structure;
- scene purpose;
- visual primitive choice;
- where charts/maps/timelines add value;
- where a source document should appear;
- where generated imagery or B-roll may add value;
- what to watch / closing frame;
- aspect and variant strategy.

It must never:

- create unsupported claims;
- invent numbers;
- change Capital Chronicle analysis;
- synthesize fake quotations;
- present generated event imagery as documentary evidence;
- create unsupported causal certainty;
- bypass source, freshness, permission, rights, or publication gates.

Every factual narration segment must bind to approved claim IDs and evidence IDs.

Non-claim language must be explicitly typed, for example:

- `context`
- `transition`
- `editorial_bridge`
- `disclaimer`
- `call_to_action`

## 7. Canonical Video Program hierarchy

The canonical Tier-2 contract is not the MP4. It is a versioned, hash-bound **Video Program** that compiles into media.

For long-form video, use a hierarchy:

```text
Video Program
├── Chapter 1
│   ├── Scene 1
│   ├── Scene 2
│   └── Scene 3
├── Chapter 2
│   ├── Scene 4
│   └── Scene 5
└── Chapter N
```

For short-form video, the same contract can use a compact single-chapter or short multi-scene program.

### 7.1 Video Program fields

Future contracts should include at least:

- video ID;
- story ID and story version;
- content version;
- input package hashes;
- director version;
- program version;
- target mode;
- target runtime;
- aspect strategy;
- frame-rate policy;
- audio policy;
- caption policy;
- motion-system version;
- source/evidence set;
- claim-binding coverage;
- rights/provenance status;
- cost budget class;
- QA status;
- revision lineage.

### 7.2 Chapter fields

Long-form chapters should include:

- chapter title;
- narrative objective;

### 7.3 Scene fields

## 9. Generative media policy

Generative video is optional enrichment, not architectural foundation.

Generated B-roll may be used only when it:

- materially improves comprehension or production quality;
- does not impersonate factual documentary footage;
- has provider/model provenance;
- fits the production cost budget;
- passes visual QA;
- is hash-bound;
- has compatible rights/terms for intended use.

Every generated asset must be replaceable without rewriting the whole video.

If generated-video providers fail, the factory must degrade to deterministic primitives and rights-cleared assets rather than block every video.

## 11. Rendering architecture

Preferred architecture:

```text
Video Program
→ Chapter/Scene compiler
→ responsive composition layer
→ Remotion-style programmable motion renderer
→ scene/chapter cache
→ FFmpeg assembly / finishing / mux / transcode
→ ffprobe deterministic verification
```

Remotion is the preferred current implementation candidate for parameterized React-based motion composition and reusable motion systems, but the canonical Video Program must not depend on Remotion-specific APIs.

The renderer must remain replaceable.

FFmpeg/ffprobe remain first-class media infrastructure for encoding, scaling, crop/pad, overlays, subtitles, audio composition, muxing, transcode, frame extraction, stream/container inspection, and final deterministic verification.

Do not build a custom codec/transcoder.

### 11.1 Long-form selective rendering

A 15–45 minute video must not require full rerender for every small correction.

The architecture must support:

```text
scene render cache
→ chapter render cache
→ proxy master
→ targeted patch
→ selective scene/chapter rerender
→ final assembly
```

A typo or chart adjustment in Chapter 4 should not force a blind regeneration of every scene in a 45-minute program.

## 12. Audio, narration, and soundtrack

Use a provider-neutral narration contract.

Every narration artifact should record:

- script/version hash;
- provider;
- voice/model identifier;
- language;
- pronunciation overrides;
- output audio hash;
- duration;
- usage/cost metadata where returned;
- provider provenance;
- rights/consent state where applicable.

Support a primary TTS provider, fallback providers, and optional human voiceover later without making any provider the architectural authority.

When only a bounded script segment changes, prefer line/scene-level narration regeneration rather than regenerating the entire 45-minute audio track.

Soundtrack defaults should be owned music, explicitly licensed production music, explicitly reusable audio, or no soundtrack. Music rights are first-class provenance and must never be an untracked ingredient.

## 13. Render and revision pipeline

The production path should be:

1. Video Program validation;
2. chapter/scene graph validation;
3. claim-binding validation;
4. asset readiness and rights validation;
5. narration generation/alignment;
6. low-cost proxy scene/chapter renders;
7. proxy master;
8. deterministic media QA;
9. multimodal visual critique;
10. structured scene/chapter defects;
11. bounded graph/asset patch;
12. selective rerender;
13. final 16:9 master and short-form variants;
14. final deterministic QA;
15. immutable package creation.

Do not repeatedly render expensive full-resolution 45-minute masters when proxy or selective renders can validate the defect.

## 14. QA model

Tier-2 uses two QA layers.

### 14.1 Deterministic QA

Verify at minimum:

- required output exists;
- expected duration tolerance;
- resolution/aspect;
- codec/container;
- frame rate;
- required audio stream;
- captions/subtitle artifacts;
- claim/script binding completeness;
- rights/provenance completeness;
- asset hashes;
- required disclosures and source credits;
- no missing scene media;
- no untracked generated asset;
- expected chapter/scene count;
- render hashes;
- no unauthorized network or public action during local-only modes.

### 14.2 Multimodal visual critic

Inspect the proxy/final render or representative frame/time samples for:

- clipped or unreadable text;
- caption collisions;
- bad hierarchy;
- pacing defects;
- dead scenes;
- excessive visual repetition;
- awkward transitions;
- chart illegibility;
- bad crop;
- 16:9 / 9:16 composition failure;
- narration/visual mismatch;
- branding inconsistency;
- synthetic-media presentation risk;
- chapter rhythm failure;
- retention-risk stretches in long-form video.

The critic returns structured defects. It does not directly mutate final media.

Revision occurs through Video Program / Chapter Graph / Scene Graph / asset patches followed by selective rerender.

## 15. Bounded revision

Automatic creative revision must have finite budgets.

The product doctrine is:

```text
initial proxy
→ critique
→ bounded revision
→ critique
→ optional final bounded revision
→ accept / block / operator escalation
```

Do not build an open-ended loop that keeps asking models to improve a video indefinitely.

Exact numeric retry limits should be calibrated from implementation evidence, latency, and cost rather than fabricated in this planning document.

## 16. Long-form and short-form relationship

One governed story may produce one long-form program and multiple short-form programs:

```text
ONE CANONICAL STORY
    |
    +-- LONG-FORM 15–45M 16:9
    |
    +-- SHORT A: core hook / key finding
    +-- SHORT B: chart or data angle
    +-- SHORT C: consequence / what-to-watch
```

Shorts can reuse long-form evidence, chapters, narration fragments, and assets, but they must be independently directed and compiled.

They may have different:

- hooks;
- scene ordering;
- timing;
- typography;
- chart framing;
- number of simultaneous elements;
- CTA;
- metadata.

Preserve factual and evidence identity while allowing true platform-native editorial adaptation.

## 18. Provenance doctrine

Internal ContentOps manifests and hashes remain canonical authority.

Design Tier-2 packages to be **C2PA-ready** so future provenance can represent composed assets, ingredients, actions, transformations, generated assets, and content bindings.

C2PA embedding is not a first vertical-slice blocker.

## 19. Platform architecture

Initial target families:

- YouTube long-form;
- YouTube Shorts;
- TikTok;
- Instagram Reels;
- optional Facebook Reels / future compatible surfaces.

Do not hardcode today's platform limits into timeless North-Star invariants.

At implementation/runtime, re-verify official provider documentation and exact account capabilities.

Current 2026-08-07 research is evidence, not timeless authority:

- YouTube supports resumable uploads and upload-state recovery for large video transfers.
- Standard YouTube channels currently classify eligible square/vertical videos up to three minutes as Shorts.
- TikTok Content Posting APIs expose upload/publish status and creator-specific maximum post duration; current developer upload documentation caps developer-sent video at ten minutes, so 15–45 minute long-form is not assumed to be a TikTok destination.
- Public upload/API behavior may depend on app review, project verification, account permission, privacy, and provider policy.

Therefore the initial product mapping is expected to be:

- 15–45 minute long-form: YouTube long-form and any future verified compatible destination;
- short-form: YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels, and compatible future surfaces.

## 20. Publication and readback

Tier-2 publication must extend, not duplicate, the canonical ContentOps doctrine:

```text
exact authorized media package
→ immutable publication intent
→ provider upload
→ upload/processing status
→ platform object identity
→ public/private/unlisted state verification
→ strict readback
→ reconciliation
→ performance observation
```

No video adapter may create an independent approval engine, outbox, retry system, unknown-write policy, durable store, or incident model.

Unknown uploads must never be blindly retried.

Known successful objects must never be duplicated because local process state was lost.

## 21. Cost and operational economics

Tier-2 is a premium differentiator only if production economics remain measurable and controllable.

Measure cost per video across:

- Director/model calls;
- critic/model calls;
- TTS;
- generated images;
- generated-video seconds;
- render compute;
- storage;
- transfer;
- provider/API calls;
- operator interactions;
- revision cycles.

Generated video should have an explicit cost budget because it may dominate variable cost.

Do not invent budget thresholds in the North Star. Measure real production first, then calibrate policy.
