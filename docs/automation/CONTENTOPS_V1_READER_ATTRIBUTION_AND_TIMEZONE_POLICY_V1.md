## Reader-facing default

Do not require a visible Sources/References/Notes section or linked citation after
each paragraph. Omit routine source lists, repeated links and ritual reporting-cutoff
footers when the article can remain accurate, useful and properly attributed without
them. The default is clean editorial copy, not a bibliography or an internal receipt.
This applies to canonical V1 articles and derivative packages, manual and scheduled.

Preserve concise natural attribution where it materially qualifies the claim, identifies
whose report or opinion it is, or distinguishes reporting from independent confirmation.
For example, a relay citing another publication must not become Capital Chronicle's
independent confirmation merely because a separate source-link block is omitted.
Use the amount of attribution the actual claim needs, without mechanical repetition.

Retain credits, source identification or links where the admitted reuse terms actually
require them, or a specific quotation/material claim needs them to avoid misleading the
reader. Jim's canonical photo-credit direction and existing admitted derivative-linking
route remain effective. Do not invent a permission or weaken the actual use restriction.
Chart meaning, metric, unit, period, estimate/actual status and essential context remain
readable even when a discretionary source-link footer is omitted.

Full source/claim/span, origin, evidence dates, cutoff, revision, calculation, rights and
native-model provenance remain in the existing internal owners. This presentation policy
does not delete evidence, erase uncertainty, change source clocks or grant redistribution.
The writer/compiler must not automatically reinsert a Sources block solely to satisfy an
obsolete template or presentation-only validator.

## Public dates and times

Omit a ritual exact-time footer when it adds no reader value. Preserve a date/time when
material to the event, timeliness, uncertainty or the meaning of a dated statement.
When an exact public timestamp is useful, default to U.S. Eastern Time using the IANA
zone `America/New_York`, with the date's actual `EST` or `EDT` label. `UTC` is an accepted
international alternative when clearer; a source-local zone may remain when meaningful.
Do not default public article copy to Bangkok/Saigon or ambiguous abbreviations.

Convert the original instant with real daylight-saving rules. A display conversion is
not a new source, analysis, publication or market-refresh time. Do not replace an old
cutoff with today's date. Existing UTC storage/source clocks, production-day identities
and the four approved Asia/Bangkok schedules are unchanged.
