# Capital Chronicle ContentOps V2 — Retention-Native Video Factory North Star V1

Authority date: 2026-08-12
Product authority: `CONTENTOPS_V2_RETENTION_NATIVE_VIDEO_FACTORY_OWNER_DIRECTION_V1`

## 1. Mission

Build Capital Chronicle ContentOps V2 into an autonomous, evidence-governed media growth engine capable of producing professional YouTube hero/mid/long-form video, YouTube Shorts, and TikTok-native short-form that earns attention without sacrificing factual integrity, rights discipline, or Capital Chronicle analytical authority.

The product objective is not to guarantee that any individual upload will become viral or trend. The objective is to create a repeatable system that increases the probability of breakout distribution and durable audience growth by consistently selecting better stories, packaging stronger promises, producing retention-native edits, learning from real audience behavior, and refusing weak or unsafe publication opportunities.

Capital Chronicle should become recognizable as a high-trust, high-signal financial/economic media brand whose videos feel native to modern attention environments while remaining visibly more rigorous than generic finance-content channels.

## 2. Product promise

The completed system should answer, every day:

1. What current story is worth turning into video?
2. Why would a viewer care now?
3. What is the most compelling truthful hook and narrative arc?
4. What evidence, people, documents, charts, maps, locations, illustrations, and audio make the story understandable and memorable?
5. What edit should be made for YouTube versus Shorts versus TikTok?
6. Is the package actually engaging, legible, rights-safe, and evidence-bound?
7. Should it publish, defer, or abstain?
8. What did real viewers do after publication?
9. What specific creative, packaging, timing, or portfolio policy should change next time?

No-publication, `VIDEO_NOT_SELECTED`, `VIDEO_BLOCKED`, and `DEFERRED` are valid product outputs. The system must never manufacture filler merely to hit a posting quota.

## 4. Core product doctrine

### 4.1 Story first, renderer second

A renderer cannot rescue a weak story. Video eligibility must score materiality, evidence strength, novelty, human/institutional stakes, conflict or change, narrative depth, visualizability, consequences, document/data richness, shelf life, current demand, portfolio concentration, platform fit, and production cost.

A thin data story must not be stretched into a 15–45 minute documentary. A story that supports only a 45-second short or a four-minute explainer should stop there. Duration is earned by narrative depth.

### 4.2 Engagement is presentation authority, not truth authority

The Engagement Director may optimize curiosity, sequencing, tension, payoff, pacing, visual rhythm, packaging, and CTA. It may not invent causes, claims, numbers, quotes, events, predictions, recommendations, controversy, fear, greed, FOMO, or human stakes that are not supported by evidence.

The system should prefer truthful forms of engagement:

- contradiction;
- consequence;
- mechanism;
- uncertainty;
- evidence reveal;
- what changed;
- what the headline missed;
- what the record can and cannot establish.

### 4.3 Beat-driven editing, not animated slides

The rejected prototypes proved that premium typography plus entrance animation is still a slideshow when the main frame stays static through narration.

Every substantive scene must support multiple narration-linked beats. The primary visual should continue telling the story with captions hidden.

A beat may establish, reveal, compare, highlight, annotate, punch in, cut away, trace a chart, move through a timeline, transition to a person/location/document, or deliver a payoff. Motion must clarify narrative meaning, not decorate idle time.

### 4.4 Audio is half of the creative product

Voice selection, prosody, pronunciation, pause structure, music, SFX, ducking, loudness, and scene joins are first-class creative decisions. Narration-only can be a safe fallback but is not automatically a professional-quality PASS for an engagement-oriented package.

### 4.5 Platform-native editing

YouTube hero/mid/long-form, YouTube Shorts, and TikTok are separate editorial outputs sharing evidence and brand authority, not one master blindly resized or cropped.

Short-form should reach value faster, change visual state more frequently, use shorter captions, and exploit portrait composition intentionally. Long/mid-form should sustain curiosity through narrative arcs, chapter re-hooks, richer asset sequences, and evolving audio states.

### 4.6 Deterministic truth and rendering infrastructure

Structured contracts, factual charts, arithmetic, source bindings, rights gates, hashes, caching, rendering, muxing, package verification, upload state, readback, and recovery should remain deterministic wherever practical.

Strong models should be used where semantic judgment has high marginal value: opportunity selection, narrative direction, difficult research synthesis, creative direction, multimodal critique, and exceptional correction.

### 4.7 Frontier-outside-the-loop economics

V2 must not depend on a frontier coding/reasoning agent staying inside the entire production control loop.

The canonical model is:

```text
shared newsroom intelligence
→ compact governed story packet
→ cheap/bounded semantic decisions
→ deterministic production
→ deterministic QA
→ bounded multimodal review
→ frontier escalation only when material quality risk justifies it
```

This doctrine is supported by actual ContentOps production evidence: recent real newsroom decisions consumed more than one million model tokens before article or video production began. The architecture therefore amortizes discovery, clustering, novelty, evidence, and memory across articles, newsletters, social packages, and video rather than paying to rediscover the same world for every video.

Provider prices, quotas, and model names are implementation-time variables, not constitutional assumptions. The product must remain provider-neutral where practical and cost-aware at the portfolio level.

### 4.8 Quality is measured by actual media and actual viewers

Unit tests and package hashes can prove technical correctness; they cannot prove an engaging video.

Professional visual/audio PASS belongs to Jim/ChatGPT after inspecting actual MP4/audio artifacts. Later, production heuristics must be calibrated against platform retention and engagement data rather than treated as universal internet truths.

## 5. Required creative contracts

V2 should converge on these renderer-independent contracts.

### `VideoOpportunity`

Carries story identity, evidence readiness, novelty, materiality, narrative depth, visualizability, asset availability, demand/trend context, shelf life, portfolio concentration, estimated production cost, eligible formats, and final selection status.

### `EngagementBrief`

Carries target audience, viewer problem, core promise, hook, pattern interrupt, central tension, curiosity/open loops, payoff checkpoints, re-hooks, pacing map, emotional register, CTA, binge/next-content target, and packaging promise.

### `VideoProgram`

Carries canonical video identity, story/version lineage, evidence hashes, format, duration policy, chapter/scene/beat graph references, asset and audio plans, platform variants, rights/provenance state, semantic content identity, QA/revision state, and package identity. It must remain renderer-neutral.

### `NarrativeBeatGraph`

Each beat carries beat ID, scene/chapter, time budget, narrative role, narration span, claim/evidence bindings, open-loop/payoff relation, required visual purpose, on-screen text intent, and transition intent.

### `EditDecisionGraph` / `MotionBeatGraph`

Carries cut/reveal/hold/reframe/punch-in/zoom/crop/split-screen/chart trace/annotation/map/timeline/entity cutaway/document focus/transition decisions. A scene may contain several evolving visual states.

### `AssetPlan`

Carries asset purpose, source, rights/license, attribution, hash, synthetic/documentary classification, crop variants, and exact beat usage.

Asset classes include official/source documents, deterministic charts, maps, timelines, real rights-cleared entity photos, institution/location imagery, filings, quotes, generated conceptual illustrations, diagrams, historical/contextual stills, and restrained B-roll.

Generated real-person documentary imagery is forbidden.

### `AudioPlan`

Carries narrator/provider/voice, pronunciation overrides, prosody/emphasis, pause map, word/phrase timing, music bed states, SFX cues, ducking, loudness target, peak target, audio rights, and scene/chapter transitions.

### `PlatformVariantPlan`

Defines independent package/edit requirements for YouTube hero/mid/long-form, YouTube Shorts, and TikTok native short-form.

### `RetentionDiagnostics`

Carries hook timing, payoff timing, meaningful visual-change intervals, static-run diagnostics excluding caption-only changes, asset diversity, caption safe-zone/line checks, audio-bed coverage, loudness/peak state, re-hook spacing, open-loop/payoff closure, promise-delivery checks, and later platform-retention mapping.

## 6. Initial quality hypotheses

These are internal starting targets, not universal factual claims. They must be calibrated with real channel data.

### Short-form

- hook visible/audible in roughly 0–1 second;
- first concrete payoff within roughly 8–12 seconds;
- meaningful visual beat roughly every 1.5–4 seconds when editorially useful;
- unjustified primary-visual static run no longer than roughly four seconds;
- captions normally no more than two lines;
- approximately 8–15 distinct visual states in a 30–60 second package where the story supports them;
- music/SFX coverage through most of the package except deliberate silence.

### Mid/long-form

- packaging promise/payoff preview within roughly 10–20 seconds;
- first substantive payoff within roughly 30–60 seconds;
- meaningful visual evolution roughly every 4–8 seconds;
- unjustified primary-visual static run no longer than roughly eight seconds;
- re-hook/open-loop renewal roughly every 20–45 seconds;
- at least four relevant asset classes when story depth supports them;
- chapter-level audio and visual identity.

### Audio

Initial mastering target for finished web/social packages:

- integrated loudness around -16 LUFS ±1;
- true peak at or below approximately -1.5 dBTP;
- no clipping;
- clean joins/fades;
- narration remains intelligible over music/SFX.

## 7. Story modes

The factory must prove that one brand system can express multiple editorial modes without becoming a template zoo.

Required initial modes:

- `ENTITY_EVENT` — person/institution/decision led;
- `DOCUMENT_REVEAL` — filing, regulation, court/policy document led;
- `DATA_MECHANISM` — chart/data/mechanism led;
- `CONFLICT_TIMELINE` — trade, sanctions, policy conflict, geopolitical chronology;
- optional `EARNINGS_BREAKDOWN` when a suitable governed story exists;
- `BREAKING_UPDATE` for concise update chains.

Each mode may choose different hooks, asset mixes, pacing, motion grammar, audio arcs, and platform variants while preserving brand and truth invariants.

## 8. Growth and portfolio doctrine

The channel should develop repeatable series rather than publish disconnected one-offs. Initial series hypotheses may include:

- Breaking Explained;
- What the Headline Misses;
- One Chart, One Consequence;
- Power & Policy;
- Big Tech / Earnings Breakdown;
- Capital Chronicle Deep Dive;
- The Evidence Boundary.

These are product hypotheses, not quotas. Series should be promoted, retired, or reframed using real performance evidence.

The daily video portfolio should choose among zero or more hero/mid-form opportunities and zero or more shorts based on qualified expected value, not frequency targets alone.

## 9. Learning loop

After exact-authorized public launch, the factory should map real platform observations back to production structure:

```text
platform retention timestamp/ratio
→ video_id
→ beat_id
→ scene_id
→ asset state
→ motion state
→ caption state
→ audio state
→ hook/open-loop/payoff identity
→ probable drop/spike cause
→ bounded policy update
```

Learning may update story-mode preference, hook class, first-payoff timing, beat density, asset mix, music intensity, title/thumbnail patterns, duration, series allocation, and publication timing.

It may never modify historical evidence, claim truth, rights state, protected public objects, or published factual authority.

Small samples should produce `NO_POLICY_CHANGE`, not confident optimization.

## 10. Model and cost doctrine

ContentOps remains the product. Codex is the repository builder, not the runtime newsroom/video controller.

At runtime:

- use shared continuous intelligence once and amortize it across content outputs;
- use deterministic/local infrastructure for repeated mechanical work;
- use cheap capable models for routine bounded decisions when quality is sufficient;
- use strong multimodal models for review when needed;
- escalate to frontier-class reasoning only when expected quality value justifies marginal cost;
- measure actual provider calls/tokens/cost where available;
- maintain a monthly external-AI portfolio budget instead of enforcing an artificial equal per-video cost;
- allow exceptional flagship stories to consume more budget than routine packages when evidence and expected audience value justify it;
- never make production availability depend on a consumer coding-agent subscription quota.

The initial business target is low marginal external-AI cost and approximately 20 high-quality long/mid-form opportunities per month only when sufficient stories qualify; it is not a hard publication quota.

## 11. Safety and authority

Capital Chronicle remains analytical/numeric truth authority. ContentOps owns governed newsroom, media transformation, packaging, publication control, readback, reconciliation, performance observation, and bounded learning.

Video has zero public-write authority until an exact task and Jim explicitly authorize a scoped public cohort.

Unknown public writes fail closed. Rights uncertainty fails closed. Missing evidence remains missing. No generated real-person documentary imagery. No fabricated screenshots, quotes, documents, events, charts, or market facts.

Trend and engagement signals may influence priority, framing, timing, and packaging. They never establish factual truth.

## 12. Acceptance doctrine

A V2 capability is not accepted because a model says it is good.

- engineering gates: deterministic tests and readback;
- media gates: actual rendered MP4/audio inspected by Jim/ChatGPT;
- provider gates: exact response/readback evidence;
- public gates: exact object identity, processing state, publication/readback/reconciliation;
- learning gates: real measured audience observations with sample-size caveats.

One implementation, one independent audit, and at most one bounded correction per task. If the same product defect persists after the allowed correction, change architecture rather than entering an audit loop.
