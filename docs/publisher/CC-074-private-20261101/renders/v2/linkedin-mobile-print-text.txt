The Fed held on October 28. Both dissenters
wanted a hike.
Why it matters: Capital Chronicle's reading is
that the decision and its implementation
answer different questions. The vote
establishes the target range and the direction
of disagreement. The companion note sets out
the rates and instructions used to implement
that stance. A reader needs both documents
to distinguish the policy choice from its
operating settings.
Context: The strongest countercase to a
looming-hike interpretation is the decision
itself: ten members approved keeping the
range unchanged. The two dissenters'
preference is evidence of disagreement at this
meeting, not a forecast of the next vote.
Reading the split as a timetable for higher
rates would give it a meaning the vote alone
cannot establish.
