What gold, the euro and the S&P did after each CPI since 2024

A historical CPI reference through 2026-10-14: early moves, later closes and gaps across gold, the euro, the S&P and the other markets.

We think CPI history is most useful when the first move and the close stay separate. Our calculations show why the horizon and the sample deserve as much attention as the surprise label.

The latest release covered is 2026-10-14. The later November release observation, frozen map and result are absent from this edition.

So what: We get a clearer comparison by checking the price series, horizon and observation count together.

The first move and the close

In the HistData gold sample, cool headline CPI prints against Investing's forecast had a mean change of -0.0175% after 5 minutes. At the close, the mean was +0.9112%. Both use 3 / 3 observations.

For hot prints in that same sample, the mean was -0.2410% after 5 minutes and -0.6731% at the close, using 4 / 4 observations.

The hot group's next-close mean was +0.1313%, using 3 / 4 observations. The smaller sample means we cannot read that positive average as a reversal by every release.

Dukascopy's euro series rose at the first interval and fell by the close on 2026-01-13. Its S&P index proxy did too.

We see changing descriptions of the sample across horizons. Those changes do not isolate CPI's effect, and a historical mean cannot forecast the next CPI.

How to read the comparisons

Every return starts from the exact price close at the release. The endpoints are 5 minutes, 30 minutes, 4 p.m. ET and the following weekday's 4 p.m. ET close.

Close and next close share that release-time baseline. The next-close column is not a return measured from the first close.

A dash means the exact endpoint is absent. It is not a zero return, and we do not fill it with an estimate.

Gold has 24 / 33 observations after 5 minutes; the euro has 12 / 33. WTI has 0 / 33 at that horizon and 2 / 33 after 30 minutes.

What hot and cool mean

The BLS figures are seasonally adjusted monthly first prints from each dated release. Unavailable first prints remain blank; revised series do not replace them.

Hot, in-line and cool compare that first print with the named source's forecast. We keep headline and core separate, and Investing and Myfxbook separate.

The desk's yardstick uses the prior 36 surprises' median absolute deviation times 1.4826, with at least 12 observations. It expresses each surprise against that source's historical spread.

A blank yardstick can mean too few prior observations or zero dispersion. An unavailable forecast remains unclassified, rather than being treated as in line.

Some forecasts were retrieved later, so the classifications do not prove what was known before the release. Forecasts saved before a release are identified separately.

Which prices the tables measure

The gold and currency histories retain their provider labels. The S&P rows distinguish index proxies from the Yahoo Finance futures proxy.

Bitcoin's Binance Bitcoin/USDT and Yahoo Finance Bitcoin/USD proxies remain separate. WTI uses the Yahoo Finance CL futures proxy, with the gaps shown in the coverage table.

Instrument references are GC gold futures, ES S&P futures, 6E euro futures, 6J yen futures, CL WTI futures and MBT Bitcoin futures.

These names locate the markets. They do not transfer any table's measured return to another contract or ETF; each price-series label defines what was measured.

The reaction, coverage, first-print, surprise and average tables belong together. Read each summary's n / eligible alongside its forecast source, CPI measure, price series, horizon, mean and median.
