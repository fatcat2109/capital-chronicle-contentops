# Capital Chronicle's pre-payrolls review gives oil a supply-risk test



Capital Chronicle's November 6 pre-payrolls follow-up separates oil's supply-risk explanation from the dollar mechanism it sees in the euro. Gold's divergence challenges a uniform dollar account. The useful distinction is the different test attached to each interpretation. [[SOURCE:SOURCE_1]]

**Oil:** The review attributes WTI's move against equities to Hormuz supply risk, without independently confirming that geopolitical premise. Its conditional oil thesis needs the contracting range to break upward. If a ceasefire or agreement is confirmed, the review's supply thesis would no longer hold; de-escalation could reprice the risk premium. [[SOURCE:SOURCE_1]]

**Euro:** Capital Chronicle links the dollar bid to the risk impulse after the November 5 U.S. equity shock. For the euro, it retains two countercases: squaring of crowded short euro positions before payrolls and the rates channel that had weakened the dollar in the November 5 U.S. session. If the rates channel reasserts itself, the dollar-risk explanation would face a competing force. [[SOURCE:SOURCE_1]]

**Gold:** The review says gold made no lower low and had stopped following the dollar leg. That divergence is the review's test of a uniform dollar explanation. [[SOURCE:SOURCE_1]]

Capital Chronicle's dated review provides analysis, not independently confirmed event facts or a performance record. [[SOURCE:SOURCE_1]]
