# SEC's November 9 data roundtable: uses, risks and costs

The SEC has announced a November 9 public roundtable on machine-understandable data and Super Intelligence (SI), including their uses, risks and costs. For readers, the distinction is between hearing evidence about those uses and assuming a discussion amounts to a policy change. [[SOURCE:SOURCE_1]]

## What the SEC has announced

In its October 19 announcement, the Securities and Exchange Commission scheduled the roundtable for November 9, 2026, from 9 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. EST at its headquarters, 100 F Street, N.E., Washington, D.C. [[SOURCE:SOURCE_1]]

The event will be open to the public, although in-person capacity may be limited and visitors will face security checks. The SEC says the discussion will be streamed live on SEC.gov, with a recording available later. [[SOURCE:SOURCE_1]]

## What to listen for

The SEC says feedback will help staff understand how the market and investing public use machine-understandable data for SI solutions, and how the Commission might better facilitate SI. [[SOURCE:SOURCE_1]]

Listen for concrete examples of use, a clear account of risks and costs, and any stated next steps. These are watchpoints for the discussion, not promised agenda items or conclusions. [[SOURCE:SOURCE_1]]

## What this does not establish

The countercase is that this may remain a listening exercise. The announcement describes a discussion and a request for feedback; it supplies no quantified benefits, cost estimates or adopted policy. [[SOURCE:SOURCE_1]]

The SEC's chief economist, Joshua White, put high-quality information at the center of the announcement. Whether the discussion produces useful evidence on that subject remains to be seen. [[SOURCE:SOURCE_1]]
